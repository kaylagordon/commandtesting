FAQ

How often is testing done?

Under NFPA 1910 (formerly NFPA 1911), in-service aerial devices and fire pumps are inspected and performance-tested at least annually. Ground ladders are tested to NFPA 1930 (formerly NFPA 1932), which also uses an annual service test. Aerial nondestructive testing is a separate interval, not the same visit as every annual test. The edition your authority having jurisdiction has adopted is the one that applies to your department.

What is the five-year aerial NDT?

NFPA 1910 (formerly NFPA 1911) calls for nondestructive testing of an aerial device at intervals not exceeding five years, in addition to the annual inspection and operational test. The same standard calls for NDT after events such as overload, impact, or heat exposure, and after repairs that affect the structure. The examination is performed by NDT Level II technicians with ASNT Level III oversight.

What happened to NFPA 1911 and NFPA 1932?

NFPA 1911 is now published as NFPA 1910. NFPA 1932 is now published as NFPA 1930. A policy or bid that still names the older number is using the former designation. We test to the current NFPA text and we still cite the former numbers so those specifications match. Newer apparatus are built to NFPA 1900; the annual in-service tests remain NFPA 1910 and, for ground ladders, NFPA 1930.

How long does a visit take?

It depends on the number of aerial devices, the ground-ladder footage, and whether a pump test is included. We will not guess a duration from the truck type alone. When you request a quote, tell us the scope and we will schedule the time the work needs.

What does the department provide?

The apparatus and ladders to be tested, a safe place to work, and a person who can position the truck when it has to be moved. For a pump test, a water source and a place to discharge water. We bring the test equipment. Pump testing is available in central Ohio and at apparatus manufacturers, not as a nationwide stop on an aerial trip.
